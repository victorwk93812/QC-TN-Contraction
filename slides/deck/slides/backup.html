B1. The kernel trick: two routes to the same scalar

\[ K \succeq 0:\ \sum_{j,k}c_jc_kK(x_j,x_k)\ge 0\ \ \forall c,\qquad \Lambda(x)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^N\phi(x_i)\ \Rightarrow\ \Lambda(x)^\top\Lambda(x')=\prod_{i=1}^N\phi(x_i)^\top\phi(x'_i) \]
A product feature map is a rank-1 tensor network; its inner product factorizes site by site (Mercer: a positive semidefinite kernel is an inner product in some feature space).

B3. AutoMPO, one bond: the bond dimension is a matrix rank

\[ H=H_{\le n}\otimes\mathbb 1+\mathbb 1\otimes H_{>n}+\sum_{a,b}M^{(n)}_{ab}\,L_a\otimes R_b,\qquad D_n=2+\operatorname{rank}M^{(n)} \]
The two extra states carry "nothing started yet" and "already finished". Compressing M by an SVD gives the minimal MPO bond dimension for that cut.

B4. The MPO as a finite automaton

\[ W_n=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb 1&0&0\\ C_n&A_n&0\\ D_n&B_n&\mathbb 1\end{pmatrix},\qquad H=\langle f|\,W_1W_2\cdots W_N\,|s\rangle \]
Rows: state at bond n−1, columns: state at bond n. Every path from s to f through the automaton spells one term of H (environment induction). For exponential couplings the middle block is a single state with A = e^{−1/ξ}·𝟙, so D = 3 at every bond (B5).
