Local improvement (Tamaki): width is a max, so fix the worst bag

Start: a random elimination order on the 5×5 grid, width .

Repeat: take the widest bag (coral) and its neighbours in the tree; decompose that region (purple) exactly; splice it back.

Width → → → = tw(5×5).

\[ \width(T)=\max_t |B_t|-1 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{re-solve the region } R \ni \arg\max_t|B_t|:\ \ \text{torso}(R) = G[\textstyle\bigcup_{t\in R}B_t] \;+\; \text{cliques on its separators} \]
Width is decided by one bag, so improving anything else is wasted. Tamaki's heuristic (PACE 2017 winner) looks at the subtree of the current decomposition around the largest bag and runs exact treewidth, exact pathwidth and a greedy heuristic on the corresponding subgraph, round robin. Our educational version (traces/td.py): region = widest bag plus its tree neighbours (10–12 vertices here); the torso adds a clique on each separator to the rest of the tree, so any decomposition of the torso can be spliced back; we solve it exactly by subset DP. Three rounds take the width from 8 to 5, which is the treewidth of the 5×5 grid. Every intermediate decomposition is validated in tests.

Why exact solvers enumerate separators. Minimal triangulations are made of minimal separators of \(G\) (Parra–Scheffler 1997); their maximal cliques are the potential maximal cliques, and treewidth is computable wherever those can be listed efficiently (Bouchitté–Todinca 2001).
Jdrasil (Bannach, Berndt, Ehlers; PACE 2016/2017): a library of reduction rules, upper-bound heuristics and exact algorithms that can be combined; the heuristic mode does up to 30 greedy runs, graph reduction, then stochastic greedy permutations and local search, and prints its best decomposition on SIGTERM. Exact solvers use the separator view from deep dive 2: optimal decompositions are assembled from minimal separators. Verdict on our circuit (data/S44.json, 10 s per run, 3 seeds, decompositions validated, converted to an order by perfect elimination and then to a pairwise tree): Tamaki reaches width 8 against MinFill's 10, but Jdrasil's width-9 decomposition gives the cheapest tree (rank 7, log₂ cost 12.64). Narrower is not always cheaper — the theme of Act IV. The paper runs both solvers as black boxes; their modes and time limits are not stated.
