Communities: blocks of tensors joined by only a few bonds

Two wheels of tensors each, joined by two bonds (x1, x2).

Best split found: purple | teal, modularity Q = .

Q compares the bonds inside each block with what random wiring of the same degrees would give.

\[ Q=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i,j}\Big[A_{ij}-\frac{k_ik_j}{2m}\Big]\,\delta(c_i,c_j) \]
Girvan–Newman also builds the plan top-down, but it splits the network where clusters are weakly linked, not where cuts are small. Q is the fraction of edges inside communities minus the fraction expected if edges were rewired at random keeping degrees (k_i). Here the two-block split has Q = 0.389 (data/F20.json, networkx modularity). Formula in this A_ij form: Clauset, Newman, Moore, PRE 70:066111 (2004), Eq. 4; Newman–Girvan PRE 69:026113 (2004) define the same Q as Tr e − ||e²||. Paper: one of the seven generators is "a Girvan–Newman community-detection heuristic" (arXiv 2608.05819 Sec 5.1); which variant, stopping rule and whether bond dimensions weight betweenness are not stated in the paper.

The algorithm: delete the bond that most shortest paths use, repeat

\[ c_B(e)=\sum_{\{s,t\}}\frac{\sigma_{st}(e)}{\sigma_{st}}\qquad(\sigma_{st}=\#\text{ shortest } s\text{--}t \text{ paths},\ \sigma_{st}(e)=\#\text{ of those using } e) \]
Two removals per click. First removal: bridge A1–B4 (the two bridges share the 25 cross-block pairs, the tie goes to the smaller name); then A2–B3 carries all 25 pairs and its removal splits the network: Q jumps to 0.389. After that the wheels are taken apart. Betweenness is recomputed after every removal (Brandes 2001: O(nm) per pass on unweighted graphs), so Girvan–Newman costs O(m²n) (Girvan–Newman, PNAS 99(12), 2002). The dendrogram on the right gains a node at each split, top-down. Ties are broken by the smallest edge name; the trace is data/F20.json.

Read bottom-up, the dendrogram is a contraction tree

Each split, read backwards, is one pairwise contraction. Node label = rank of that intermediate.

GN tree: max rank , cost .

Best possible (subset DP): max rank , cost .

\[ \text{GN tree } T_{GN}:\quad \max_{e\in T_{GN}} |\delta(e)| = \text{its carving width} \;\ge\; \cw(G) \]
Every split of a component into two parts, read from the leaves up, contracts the two parts into one tensor; the dendrogram is directly a pairwise plan, so GN lives in the carving picture (no elimination order needed). One click per merge, bottom-up. On the two wheels the GN tree reaches rank 6 and costs 740 (χ = 2); the optimal tree (carving DP over all trees) reaches rank 4 and costs 388. Splitting the bridges first is the right top-level move; the inner splits of each wheel are not chosen for small cuts.

Betweenness and modularity reward splitting where few shortest paths cross — often a small cut, but not the same objective: no bound on the width of \(T_{GN}\), and \(O(m^2 n)\) time (Brandes 2001 per pass).
All trees evaluated in the same carving model (tree_stats): GN on the tensor graph; MinFill and FlowCutter-lite ND on the index graph, converted with order_to_tree. GN matches the optimum on the ring and the bowtie, but on the two wheels (its showcase), the grids and the circuit it is wider and costlier than MinFill. Its quality is an empirical question; here it never wins. Data: data/F21.json; tests/test_figures.py asserts every cell.
