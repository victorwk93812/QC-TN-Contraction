Reproduction · release and paper

The release next to the paper: what differs

Labels and timing

  • No GPU timing code. The plan files' time column comes from the release's mock run: index bookkeeping, no tensor data. Our run of that function follows it step by step (Spearman median over plans).
  • Hyperindex form, not dense. A labelled plan (, speedup ) needs a rank- dense intermediate, rank in QXTns' hyperindex form. The paper describes dense pairwise GEMMs.
  • Identical plans, different labels. plans that are step for step their circuit's MinFill plan have speedups , not 1.
  • MinFill's own speedup isn't 1 in some circuits, against the paper's definition; where the release's measurement file covers them, it has MinFill at 1 and the same order.

Ranker and evaluation

  • Each Table 6 row combines two models ( and rounds for opt_ndcg); opt_ndcg's eta and subsample differ from Table 4.
  • Regret on the label \(\log(1 + \mathrm{Sp})\), not on the speedup the paper defines it on.
  • No code for Table 7, the win rate or the random and MinFill + random baselines.
  • The stored models are fitted on all circuits, test sets included.

Features, splits and plans

  • Jdrasil rows carry their circuit's MinFill features, with their own labels.
  • Split features are z-scored with a fixed scaler that isn't in the release.
  • Plan names and circuits: some plans replay on another circuit than their name says; no code for the seven generators.
  • No second GPU: the cross-GPU tables can't be checked from the release.

The decomposition re-implements QXTns contract_pair! step for step with a CUDA synchronisation and a clock after each phase: finding the contraction indices, tensor_data (gathers each operand's hyperindex groups; no operand was ever expanded or re-reduced), the einsum with its synchronisation, and the rest (building the result, updating the bond map). Its total is 0.86–1.14 times the library call's (median 0.98). Bars are the sum of each phase over a circuit's plans divided by the sum of all four. Text columns: the library call's median ms per step (0.5–0.7 on the pricing-call circuits, more on DJ and portfolio, whose large hyperindex groups make the search longer) and CPU time over GPU time for the same call (medians over plans). Einsums alone: OMEinsum's EinCode per step on prepared operands of the step's shapes. Arithmetic: 2^36 complex multiply-adds at 8 real operations each, at 15.7 TFLOPS FP32. The heaviest labelled plan needs 2^41.2. Overhead of 0.5–4 ms per step over hundreds to thousands of steps is 0.1 s to tens of seconds, so for nearly every labelled plan the bookkeeping is larger than the arithmetic in this stack.
