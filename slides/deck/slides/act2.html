Lemma. For a bipartition \((X, Y)\) of the tensors, the bonds crossing it form a vertex separator of the index graph between the indices internal to \(X\) and those internal to \(Y\).
Proof: an index-graph edge means one tensor carries both indices. An internal index of X lives only on tensors in X, so its neighbours are internal to X or crossing bonds; same for Y. So every path from X-internal to Y-internal passes a crossing bond. This is why the two pictures can be used interchangeably: an edge cut of size s in the tensor graph costs χ^s, exactly like a separator of size s among the indices. Data: data/F17.json, checked with separator_report.

\[ \fillin(v)=\#\{\{a,b\}\subseteq N(v): a\not\sim b\}, \qquad \width(\pi)=\max_t |N(v_t)|, \qquad \tw(G)=\min_\pi \width(\pi) \]
The game: pick a vertex, connect all its remaining neighbours, delete it. The number of edges it adds is its fill. The width of an order is the largest neighbourhood met; the treewidth is the best width over all orders. On the ring every vertex starts with fill 1 (its two neighbours are not adjacent); after one step the remaining triangle is a clique, so no more fill. Rule shown: MinFill, ties by degree then a seeded random value (seed 0, data/F06_ring.json).

Tree decomposition \((T,\{B_t\})\): \[ \textstyle\bigcup_t B_t=V;\quad \forall uv\in E\ \exists t: \{u,v\}\subseteq B_t;\quad \{t: v\in B_t\}\ \text{connected};\qquad \width=\max_t|B_t|-1 \] \[ \tw(G)=\min\{\,\omega(H)-1 : H\supseteq G,\ H \text{ chordal}\,\} \]
Every elimination order gives a tree decomposition: the bag of a step is v plus its neighbours at that time, and bags are linked in the order their vertices are eliminated. Conversely every tree decomposition gives orders of the same width. The second line: add all fill edges of an order and you get a chordal graph H whose largest clique is the largest bag; minimizing over chordal supergraphs is the same as minimizing over orders. Bag sizes here are read from the nested-dissection trace (data/F14.json): 7 at the root, own 3 + inherited 7 = 10 for the half rows, own 3 + inherited 6 = 9 for the columns, at most 8 in the leaves. Width = 10 − 1 = 9.

\[ \tw(G)\ \ge\ \max\big(\omega(G)-1,\ \operatorname{degeneracy}(G),\ k \text{ if } G \text{ has a } k\times k \text{ grid minor}\big) \]
Excluded grid theorem. (Robertson–Seymour 1986) \(\exists f:\ \tw(G)\ge f(k)\Rightarrow G\) has a \(k\times k\) grid minor; \(f\) polynomial (Chekuri–Chuzhoy 2016), now \(f(k)=O(k^{9}\operatorname{polylog} k)\) (Chuzhoy–Tan 2021).
Recall the primer (slide 5): treewidth is the smallest frontier any order can manage. To prove it is large we need certificates. A clique of size ω needs a bag of size ω whatever the order (the first of its vertices to go sees the other ω − 1), so tw ≥ ω − 1; for K5 that is 4 and it is tight. The degeneracy bound holds because any order meets, at its first vertex of a min-degree-δ subgraph, at least δ live neighbours. On a grid both bounds are tiny (ω = 2, degeneracy 2) yet tw(4×4) = 4: the certificate is the grid itself, because treewidth never increases when taking minors and tw(k×k grid) = k. The excluded grid theorem is the converse: large treewidth forces a large grid minor. Values: data/S10.json (exact DP). Sources: Robertson–Seymour GM V, JCTB 41 (1986); Chekuri–Chuzhoy, J. ACM 63(5) (2016), first polynomial bound; Chuzhoy–Tan, JCTB 146 (2021), O(g^9 polylog g); the lower bound is Ω(g² log g).

Carving width: the largest cut a contraction tree carries

((A B)(C D))
((A C)(B D))
Each label = the bonds crossing that tree edge. Best possible for the ring: carving width = max degree .
\[ \cw(G)=\min_{\text{binary trees}}\ \max_{\text{tree edges } e} |\delta(e)|\ \ge\ \Delta(G), \qquad 2(m+n+k)\le 3\,\cw\ \Rightarrow\ \text{step cost}\le \chi^{1.5\,\cw} \]
Cut a contraction tree at one of its edges: the tensors below form one side, everything else the other; the bonds crossing are the open legs of that intermediate. Carving width minimizes the largest such cut. Leaf edges count too, so cw ≥ max degree. The cost bound: at a step with inputs of rank m+k and n+k and output of rank m+n, each of the three is a tree-edge cut, so each is ≤ cw. Adding the three gives 2(m+n+k) ≤ 3 cw. Ring values from data/F04.json (subset DP, carving.carving_width_dp). Sources: cw ≥ Δ is Observation 1 in Belmonte et al., DAM 2013; the 1.5·cw cost bound is O'Gorman, TQC 2019, Thm 1 (time n·2^{1.5 ec+1}, space 2^{ec+1}, ec = carving width).

The bowtie: hub H of degree 4 on two cycles, pendants U and V. A list schedule keeps one running tensor and absorbs one tensor at a time: that is a caterpillar tree. None of the 5040 lists keeps every intermediate at rank 2; the best reaches rank 3 (cost 58). That is why the talk moves freely between the pictures.
