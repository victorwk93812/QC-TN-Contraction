Two disjoint paths prove that no single vertex separates a from h

Menger (1927). \[ \min\{|S|: S \text{ separates } u,v\}=\max\{\#\text{ internally vertex-disjoint } u\text{--}v \text{ paths}\} \] computed by max-flow min-cut (Ford–Fulkerson 1956) with unit vertex capacities.
{x, y} has size 2. Is there a separator of size 1? The two coral paths a–c–x–e–h and a–d–y–z–f–h share no inner vertex, so any separator must hit both: size ≥ 2. Menger says this certificate always exists, and max-flow finds both the cut and the paths. That is the engine inside FlowCutter (slide 36). Sources: Menger, Fund. Separator-last lemma. \[ \text{eliminate } A \text{ before } S\cup B \ \Rightarrow\ \text{no fill edge joins } A \text{ and } B; \qquad \width(\pi)=\max\big(w_A,\,w_B,\,|S|-1\big) \]
Invariant: there is never an edge between a live A vertex and a B vertex. Eliminating a ∈ A only connects neighbours of a, which lie in A ∪ S. So the two sides are independent subproblems and their widths do not add; the separator itself is a clique at the end and costs |S| − 1. Theory: small balanced separators ⟺ small treewidth

7×7 grid: n = , treewidth , middle column = √n
Robertson–Seymour (1986). \( \tw(G)\le k \Rightarrow \exists S,\ |S|\le k+1 \): every component of \(G-S\) has \(\le (n-|S|)/2\) vertices.
Dvořák–Norin (2019). Every subgraph has a \(\tfrac23\)-balanced separator of size \(\le s\ \Rightarrow\ \tw(G)\le 15s\).
Lipton–Tarjan (1979). \(G\) planar \(\Rightarrow\) a \(\tfrac23\)-balanced separator with \(|S| \le 2\sqrt2\,\sqrt n\) \(\Rightarrow \tw(G)=O(\sqrt n)\).
The two directions: bounded treewidth gives balanced separators of size tw + 1 (here 8, components ≤ 20; the middle column already does it with 7); conversely, if every subgraph has balanced separators of size s, treewidth is at most 15s (Dvořák–Norin, JCTB 137, 2019; balanced = both sides ≤ 2n/3). Planar graphs have separators of size ≤ 2√2·√n (Lipton–Tarjan, SIAM J. Appl. Math. 36, 1979; for n = 49 that bound is 19.8 while the grid needs only 7), so tw = O(√n); the treewidth consequence came later (Fomin–Thilikos give 3.182√n). Robertson–Seymour, Graph Minors II, J. Algorithms 7 (1986). So "find small balanced separators" is the right target, up to constants.

\[ \width_{\text{balanced ND}} \approx 1.5\,L, \qquad \width_{\text{one cut + inward sweeps}} = L = \tw \]
Balanced ND reaches width 45 at L = 31 (9, 21, 33, 45 at L = 7, 15, 23, 31), MinFill 41, the column sweep 31, and one balanced cut followed by inward sweeps 31. Inherited boundaries stack up: a quarter block carries its own separator plus pieces of two ancestors. George's optimality is for polynomial costs (FLOPs of sparse Cholesky); in tensor networks the width is an exponent, so a factor 1.5 in width is a factor 2^{0.5 L} in time and memory. FlowCutter: grow both sides and record every cut on the way

Max-flow \(S \to T\) gives a min cut; the smaller side absorbs its region and pierces one cut vertex. Cuts grow in size while balance improves: the Pareto front \((k,\ k(k{-}1)/2),\ k=2,\dots,7\).
Hamann and Strasser, "Graph bisection with Pareto optimization", ACM J. Exp. Algorithmics 23 (2018) (ALENEX 2016). Source = top-left corner, target = bottom-right corner, four cuts per click. Purple = source region, teal = target region, coral = current cut. The front on the 7×7 grid is the diagonal cuts: size k cuts off k(k−1)/2 vertices; the last cut, the anti-diagonal of size 7, splits 21 | 21 — as balanced as the middle column, found without asking for balance. Our version recomputes max-flow each step (traces/flowcutter_lite.py); the real tool augments incrementally (O(c·m) total), runs ~20 random s–t pairs, and for ordering does nested dissection with the minimum-expansion cut among those with ≤ 60% imbalance. In PACE 2017 the treewidth heuristic track was won by Tamaki et al.; FlowCutter was second. On the circuit's index graph (data/F19.json) the front is (3, 1), (5, 2), (6, 23): one cut of 6 bonds splits the 54 indices almost in half.

The 2×3 tensor grid from slide 9. An elimination order of the indices gives a tree decomposition (one bag per step) and, by merging the tensors that carry each eliminated index, a pairwise contraction tree (carving.order_to_tree). The conversion fixes the width but not the shape: the 3168 orders of width 3 give trees whose cost ranges from 52 to 72 (χ = 2), and they differ in how the expensive merges are arranged. That is the gap Act IV exploits: plans of equal width are not equal on a GPU. Scorecard: measured on 7×7, 20×20 and the circuit's index graph (data/SC.json). Note that on this circuit FlowCutter-lite ND (width 13) is worse than MinFill (10): balance is not the objective that matters on a small, irregular network. The ND guarantee: every bag lies inside the union of the separators on its root path.
