Four takeaways

  1. The order, not the network, decides the cost: one ring already shows rank against rank .
  2. Bottom-up greedy rules see distance-2 structure; top-down separator methods see the global cut — but balance alone does not minimize width.
  3. Width, FLOPs, memory and GPU runtime are four different objectives, and they disagree on real graphs.
  4. Ranking a diverse pool of plans beats committing to any single heuristic.
\[ \chi^{\tw(L(G))}\ \le\ \text{peak memory of the best order},\qquad \tw\ \asymp\ \text{balanced separator size},\qquad \text{runtime}=F(\text{plan shape},\ \text{hardware}) \]
One chain: contraction cost is exponential in width (Act I, Markov–Shi); width is governed by balanced separator size (deep dive 2, Robertson–Seymour, Dvořák–Norin); heuristics differ in how they find separators (deep dives 1–4); and runtime depends on plan shape beyond width (Act IV). Evidence: ring 2 vs 4 (slide 2); MinFill wider but cheaper until L = 22 (slide 26); balanced ND 1.5× wider (slide 35); Jdrasil narrower-cost but wider-width on our circuit (slide 44); the paper's ranker 60% Top-1 against 12% for MinFill-first (slide 49).
