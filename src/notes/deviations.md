## Phase 1: ranker on the published splits

**Reproduced exactly: Table 6, Top-1, Top-3, Regret1, Regret3 of both models**, in Python and with the
authors' Julia code (`experiments/julia/t06_t07.jl`), under these conditions, all from their Notebook 5:

| The paper says or implies | The code does |
|---|---|
| One model per row | Two models per row: `opt_ndcg` takes Top-1 and Regret1 from 1 round, Top-3 and Regret3 from 65; `opt_pair` from 16 and 14 |
| `opt_ndcg`: eta 0.01, subsample 0.7 (Table 4) | eta 0.02, subsample 0.8; Table 4's values never give the published Top-3 or regrets in rounds 1–300 |
| Regret on speedup, `1 - Sp_1 / Sp*` | Regret on the label, `log(1 + Sp)`. On speedup, no round matches, and MinFill's Regret1 is 0.1015, not 0.0735 |

**The stored models are not test evidence.** `models/opt_pair.json` is the Notebook 5 recipe fitted on all 225

that's a normal choice. They score far above the tables (Top-1 84–97%) for this reason, and Table 6 doesn't use them.

`experiments/p2_plans_features.py`; checks in `tests/test_plans.py`. Line numbers refer to the release at `936630a`.

**Plan files.** The release's `plans.tar.gz` holds 1663 files. 1661 parse and are reproduced byte for byte when
written back (`qctn.plans.authors`). Two have a truncated line and don't parse: `Qwalk_5` (line 1009, the two tensor

(`read_contractions_analisi_ranks_indexs`, `src/Funcions_de_Features_v_5.jl`) these lines would also fail, at the
integer parse. Two further files reuse a result name, so they aren't a valid tree: `QWALK_3_pla_Flow_cutter` (step 75)
and `QWALK_6_pla_flow_cutter` (step 1451); neither generator name is one of the seven. Four plans (`RQC_4_20_*`,
`RQC_4_60_*`) end with four disconnected roots. The line format is `print(f, ...)` in `contract_pair_rank!`
(`src/funcions_article_IA.jl`, l. 183) followed by the `@timed` seconds of a mock contraction (l. 41, 48): the last
column is a time, not a cost.

**Features: matched.** `qctn.features` reimplements `get_features_v5` (`src/Funcions_de_Features_v_5.jl`,
l. 341–394); Notebook 2 (`2_Getting_18_features_from_file.ipynb`, cell 8) calls it on each file. On the 1657 plans
that are both parsed and in `Features.xlsx`, 1651 agree on all 18 features to 1e-9 relative (in practice to rounding),
and the QFT_5 values printed in Notebook 2, cell 12, are reproduced to 1e-12. The constants and choices, all from
the code:

| | The paper states | The code does |
|---|---|---|
| ε in d_i | ε ≪ 1 (Eq. 9, App. A.3), no value | `epsilon=1.0e-6` (l. 29, 56) |
| τ in frac_tiny | "e.g. τ = 6" (App. A.6, Eq. 26) | `tau=6` (l. 131), steps with c_i ≤ c_max − 6 |
| q in topq_mean_cost | I_q = the ⌈qS⌉ largest c_i (App. A.5, Eq. 22), no value | `q=5` percent, k = `max(1, round(Int, S*q/100))` (l. 116–119): rounding to nearest (ties to even), not ceiling; the two differ on 781 of 1661 plans |

| k_i | \|S_i\| | (r_A + r_B − r_C) / 2 from the file's ranks (l. 548–549); equals the printed index count on every step |

**Features: not matched.** Six plan files give other features than their `Features.xlsx` rows, and the step counts
differ (file vs xlsx): `RQC_4_11_pla_mf` (1 vs 168), `RQC_4_20_pla_fc`, `_gn`, `_mf` (189, 189, 192 vs 263),
`RQC_4_60_pla_fc`, `_mf` (189 vs 698). The `RQC_4_20` and `RQC_4_60` files have the same step sequence and contain
outer products (45 steps with no common index, results up to rank 43). For such a step the release's reader indexes
`comptador[0]` (l. 582), which is out of bounds in Julia; we haven't run it. `Features.xlsx` must come from other
versions of these files. `RANDOM_11_pla_mf` has an xlsx row but its file doesn't parse.

**The split files' scaler: identified up to the row set.** In each split file only the 13 model features are
standardised; the other five columns are raw. With the raw values from `Features.xlsx`, each standardised column
is one affine map of the raw value, the same for both objectives and all three splits, residual ≤ 1e-14
(`results/p2_scaler.csv`; `max_asym_val` 1.6e-8, its σ is 1.5e-7). So it's a z-score with fixed μ, σ per feature.
For the integer features (`n_steps`, `max_out_rank`, `max_red_rank_val`), μ·N and the sum of squares
σ²(N − 1) + Nμ² are integers only for N = 773 (or multiples) with ddof = 1: the scaler was fitted on 773 rows, with
the sample std (so not sklearn's `StandardScaler`, which uses ddof = 0). We didn't find which 773 rows. Tried, none
reproduce μ and σ (best: training rows, μ off by up to 0.016 σ, σ off by up to 6.6%): all 1658 xlsx rows; the 1561
rows of the seven generators; the 1475 split rows, as published, deduplicated, or replaced by their own xlsx rows;
train, train + ID, train + OOD, ID, OOD alone; circuits with exactly seven plans; rows present in `measures.xlsx`;
speedup ≤ 2; ddof 0 and 1 for each. An exact-cover search over whole circuits (scipy `milp`, 60 s per pool) didn't
terminate. The release contains no scaling code (no z-score, `StandardScaler` or normalisation in `src/` or the
notebooks), and the paper mentions none (Sec. 5.3 says features are fed to XGBoost).

**Jdrasil rows in the splits carry MinFill's features.** Of the 224 `_pla_jdrasil` rows in the split files, 213 have
exactly the features of the same circuit's `_pla_mf` plan, where `Features.xlsx` (and our recomputation from the
plan files) gives the Jdrasil plan different features. Their speedups are their own (e.g. `GHZ_100`: Jdrasil 1.027,
MinFill 1.000, same features). For 7 rows Jdrasil and MinFill have equal features anyway; 4 rows match neither.
In all, 13 split rows match no xlsx row: the 4 rows of `RQC_4_6_34`, the 7 of `RQC_6_20`, `RANDOM_10_pla_jdrasil`
and `RQC_5_28_pla_jdrasil` (`p2_split_sources.csv`). The other 1249 rows equal their own xlsx row. In those 213 circuits the ranker sees two plans with identical features and
different labels.

**Circuits and networks: matched where they can be compared.** Only 17 of the 332 circuit names in the plan files
have a QASM file in the release (`dj_*`, `portfolioqaoa_*`, `pricingcall_*`; 15 of them in the splits). The others
(GHZ_n, RQC_*, QFT_*, QNN_* …) are named differently from the 257 QASM files or were built in Julia (Notebook 1_a
uses `create_ghz_circuit`), and we haven't mapped them. `qctn.circuits.authors_gates` follows `import_from_qasm` and
`process_gate_line!` (`src/funcions_qasm.jl`, l. 113, 608–821) and `qctn.tn.build` follows QXTools `convert_to_tnc`
with QXTns `push!` (gate tensors in circuit order, two-qubit gates split by SVD, then inputs, then outputs). Replaying
all 109 plans of the 17 circuits on our networks reproduces every step's three ranks and the common indices' qubit
tags, prime levels and dimensions (ITensors' random index ids can't be compared). Conventions found on the way:

- QXZoo's `GateCall2(symbol, ctrl, target)` stores its first qubit as `ctrl`, while `c_x(q_target, q_ctrl)` passes the
  target first; with the authors' `c_x(target, ctrl)` the network gets the qubits in QASM order (control first).
  `swap` and their `cu1` end up in reversed QASM order. This only affects which half of the gate gets the lower id.
- The authors' parser only reads gates on a register named `q` and skips other lines with a printed message
  (l. 821). Over the 257 QASM files it drops 38,397 `cp` and 8 `x` on the Grover `flag` register (the release has
  `replace_qasm_registers_grover` for those, l. 516, which we haven't applied) and every `u` (179), `u3` (130),
  `ccx` (68) and `cry` (12). None of these occur in the 17 compared circuits.
- QXTns `push!` (`tensor_network_circuit.jl`, l. 97) reshapes `A` instead of `B` when the SVD rank is 1, so the second
  half gets the first half's data. Ranks and ids are unaffected.

A Julia dump of the network (QXTools via PyCall) for an independent check hung at package loading on the login node
and was dropped; the replay against the plan files is the check.

MinFill row.

**Standardisation and the model.** Tree splits are invariant to increasing affine maps, so in exact arithmetic
the scaler wouldn't matter. In practice the DMatrix stores float32. Scaling the split features by 2 gives
identical predictions, but shifting by 3, or undoing the release's z-score, doesn't: at 300 rounds the Notebook 5
recipe's ID/OOD predictions differ by up to 0.30–0.41 (`opt_ndcg`) and 0.79–1.17 (`opt_pair`), and the metrics are
equal at only 3–6 (`opt_ndcg`) and 0 (`opt_pair`) of rounds 1–300. Undoing the z-score one column at a time
(`p2b_invariance.csv`) leaves predictions bit-identical for every feature whose float32 values keep their order
and ties. The change comes from the features where they don't: `max_asym_val` (raw values within about 1e-7 of 1,
below float32 resolution there: 7 distinct raw values against 30 standardised, since the release's σ is 1.5e-7),
`d_at_max_cost_val` (492 against 511), and for `opt_pair` also `costw_out_rank` (1141 against 1140). So the
standardisation changes the model only through float32 rounding. Grouped CV then picks a different round count,
and on 25 or 62 circuits that moves the metrics by as much as the corrections below.

## Phase 3: the seven generators

Numbers: `results/p3_generators.csv` (the 17 circuits with a QASM file, budgets 10 s and 60 s) and
`results/p3_generators_all.csv` (every network `qctn.catalog` builds, 10 s), regenerated by
`experiments/p3_generators.py` (about 40 min, 4 processes) and `experiments/p3_catalog.py` (about 50 min); code in
`qctn.plans.qxtools`, `.girvan_newman`, `.pace`, `.generators`; solvers built by `scripts/setup_solvers.sh`; checks in
`tests/test_generators.py`. Julia line numbers refer to the depot of the authors' Manifest: QXTools 1.0.0
(`src/contraction_planning.jl`), QXGraphDecompositions 1.0.0 (`src/treewidth.jl`, `src/LabeledGraph.jl`), QXTns,
LightGraphs, Julia 1.9.1 (`base/`).

**What the release says.** No code in the release makes the seven plans. Notebooks 1_a and 1_b (cells 14 and 13)
call `flow_cutter_contraction_plan(tnc; time=10)`; QXTools itself offers only that and `min_fill_contraction_plan`.
The paper (Sec. 5.1) says MinFill and FlowCutter "were obtained through QXTools" and names a Girvan–Newman heuristic,
"the Jdrasil heuristic solver", "the Tamaki local-improvement heuristic" and "two MinFill-based heuristics submitted by
Terrioux et al." to PACE 2017, with no parameters. What follows is reconstructed from the plan files and checked by
regenerating them.

**One pipeline for all seven.** Every plan in the release reads as an edge elimination order turned into pairwise steps
by QXTools `order_to_contraction_plan` (l. 386–448): step `I<i>` contracts the intermediates holding the two tensors of
the i-th bond in the order; bonds already inside one intermediate are skipped, leaving gaps in the `I` numbers. The
order is over the line graph of `convert_to_line_graph(tn; use_hyperedges=false)` (l. 47–63): one vertex per bond, in
the insertion order of QXTns' `bond_map`, labelled `:1, :2, …`. The generators differ only in how they find the order:

| | Order from | Here |
|---|---|---|
| `mf` | `min_fill(lg)` (`treewidth.jl` l. 165–182), as in `min_fill_contraction_plan` | Python port, Julia's tie order |
| `fc` | FlowCutter PACE 2017 tree decomposition, then `order_from_tree_decomposition` (l. 198–232) | binary, built from the jll's source revision |
| `tamaki`, `jdrasil` | the solver's tree decomposition, same conversion | PACE 2017 binaries; randomised, anytime |
| `minfill_mrs`, `minfillbg_mrs` | the solver's tree decomposition, same conversion | PACE 2017 binaries; deterministic |
| `gn` | reverse Girvan–Newman edge-removal order on the tensor graph | Python, rustworkx or networkx |

The line graph is the plain-bond one, not the hyperedge one that `flow_cutter_contraction_plan` uses by default
(`hypergraph=true`, l. 93). With hyperedges, a group of more than two tensors is contracted by netcon, whose results are
named `t<n>` by `next_tensor_id!`. The notebook's own fc plan for `dj_indep_qiskit_40` (Notebook 1_b, cell 14) has such
names and its last step is `I158`; the release's file has `I1`…`I236`, one step per bond, and no `t<n>`. In the
release, `t<n>` results occur in 39 of the 1561 plans of the seven generators (fc 6, jdrasil 19, mf 2, minfill_mrs 4,

So these candidate sets hold the same plan twice, and the ranker data two rows with identical features and different
measured speedups (compare the Jdrasil rows of the split files, phase 2b). We model it as follows: every solver is
stopped at the budget and its output read 1 s after the SIGTERM (`qctn.plans.pace.READ_DELAY`); a decomposition that is
missing or incomplete (all bags, a tree, every vertex and edge of the line graph covered) falls back to MinFill, and so
does any output of the two MinFill heuristics once they had to be stopped. They aren't anytime solvers: stopped early,
`minfill_mrs` prints nothing and `minfillbg_mrs` prints a valid but near-trivial decomposition (a few bags, width 773
on `_21` after 10 s), 1–3 s after the SIGTERM. Julia reads the file immediately; the 1 s and the discard rule are our
choices, made to match the release. With them, at 10 s, our fallbacks are exactly the release's for the MinFill
heuristics. The two `jdrasil` fallbacks are not reproduced: here Jdrasil writes a decomposition within 1 s of the
SIGTERM on `_23` and `_25`. On the authors' machine it evidently didn't. Budget 10 s follows the notebooks; the paper
gives none.

**MinFill ties.** `min_fill` takes `argmin` over a `Dict{Int,Int}` of cliqueness values, so ties go to the first key in
the Dict's slot order. That order follows Julia 1.9's `hash(::Int)` (`hash_64_64`, `base/hashing.jl`) and linear
probing after `sizehint!` (`base/dict.jl` l. 80–86, 219–225, 289–330). `eliminate!` updates the values incrementally
(`LabeledGraph.jl` l. 326–375), and LightGraphs' `rem_vertex!` renumbers the last vertex into the removed one's place.
`qctn.plans.qxtools` reproduces all three; the slot order for keys 1–20 and 1–300 is tested against Julia 1.9.1's output.

**Girvan–Newman.** There is no code for it in the release. Our version works on QXTools' `convert_to_graph` (l. 18–29:
one vertex per tensor in network order, one edge per adjacent pair). It repeatedly removes the edge of largest
unnormalised edge betweenness, recomputed after each removal, with ties going to the first edge in LightGraphs' order
(the smallest (min, max) vertex pair). The contraction order is the reverse removal order, each edge standing for its
bonds. This reproduces the release's gn plans step for step (14 of 14 here; catalog below). Ties are on exact float
equality. With any relative tolerance from 1e-12 to 1e-6, `pricingcall_…_7` and `portfolioqaoa_…_10` diverge (steps 341
and 741), so near-ties were broken by value in the authors' run. Exact-tie breaking reproduces the release only because
the betweenness values of rustworkx 0.18.1, networkx 3.7 and the authors' implementation agree to the last bit on these
networks. That is an empirical match, not a guarantee across libraries or platforms; `rtol` is a parameter. rustworkx
(Rust) is the default and networkx (pure Python, O(m²n)) the fallback; both give the same plans on `dj_40`,
`pricingcall_5` and `_7` (tested). Only the component of the removed edge is recomputed.

## Phase 4: executing and timing plans

Numbers: `results/p4_timing.csv` (one row per plan and mode), `p4_compare.csv` (per circuit), `p4_estimate.csv`,
`p4_leaves.csv`, regenerated by `experiments/p4_timing.py` (GPU, Slurm job `scripts/slurm/p4_small.sbatch`),
`experiments/julia/p4_qxtns.jl`, `experiments/p4_compare.py`, and `experiments/julia/p4_leaves.jl` with
`experiments/p4_leaves.py`; code in `qctn.execute`; checks in `tests/test_execute.py`. Release line numbers refer to
`936630a`; QXTns, QXTools and QXZoo to the versions in the authors' Manifest (QXTns `src/`, QXZoo `src/gates/`).

**The release has no GPU timing code.** Its only timed contraction is `contract_tn_rank_mock!`
(`src/funcions_article_IA.jl` l. 28–71), which wraps each `contract_pair_rank!` in `@timed` (l. 41) and writes the
seconds as the last column of the plan line (l. 48). Notebooks 1_a (cell 18) and 1_b (cell 16) call it with
`mock=true` after moving the tensors' storage to `CuArray`. With `mock=true` QXTns `contract_pair`
(`tensor_network.jl` l. 256–258) builds a result without data, so no tensor is contracted and the plan files' time
column measures host bookkeeping (index sets, hyperindex groups, the bond map), not GPU work. The notebooks say so
themselves: "we do not contract 'really' in a GPU, so the time results are negligible" (1_a cell 23, 1_b cell 21).
Nothing in `src/` or the notebooks uses BenchmarkTools, `@belapsed`, `CUDA.synchronize`, a warm-up, repeats or
Float32. The protocol is in the paper only: one warm-up and 50 measured runs, the mean reported, Float32,
`CUDA.synchronize` around each run, transfers, allocation, plan creation and JIT excluded; a plan over 10 minutes or
out of memory counts as failed. The labels come from the RTX A6000; the paper's Volta card is a V100 PCIe 32 GB,
used for the cross-GPU tables only. Ours is a V100-SXM2 16 GB.

**Element type and values in the authors' stack.** QXTools `gate_matrix` returns `Array{ComplexF64}` (`circuits.jl`
l. 75); inputs and outputs are the Float64 vector `[1, 0]` (`tensor_network_circuit.jl` l. 8–11, 159–172), so each
network is the amplitude ⟨0…0|C|0…0⟩. We cast to complex64 for timing, our reading of the paper's Float32 for complex
tensors. Building the authors' tensor values needs three behaviours of their stack, all reproduced by
`qctn.execute.leaf_arrays` (default `convention="qxtns"`):

- QXTns reshapes a two-qubit gate matrix to four indices in Julia's column-major order (`tensor_network_circuit.jl`
  l. 93), so the first entry of `gate_qubits` becomes the least significant bit. Together with the qubit order
  noted in phase 2, every two-qubit gate acts with its qubits' roles exchanged: in `cx` the QASM target is the control.
  The structure is unchanged, the values and the amplitude are not.
- The release's QASM reader registers every `u2`, `u1`, `p` and `cu1` under one fixed label
  (`funcions_qasm.jl` l. 671, 699, 727, 796), and QXZoo's `cache_gate!` (`gate_map.jl` l. 205–210) keeps the first
  matrix stored under a label. So all gates of one of these kinds get the matrix of the first one created in the
  Julia session. We take the first in the circuit; a session that read several files would differ.
- For Schmidt rank 1 the second half copies the first (phase 2; l. 97). None of the 17 circuits has such a gate.

Checked against QXTools itself: on all 17 QASM networks every leaf's hyperindex groups and stored data agree with
`convert_to_tnc` + their reader (`p4_leaves.csv`: 0 differences, data within 4e-16 up to sign), and on
`pricingcall_…_5` the amplitude equals QXTns `contract_tn!`'s (ComplexF64) to 1e-16. On the GPU, the complex64
results of all 21 plans timed in Julia agree with QXTns' ComplexF32 results to 1e-6 relative.

**Dense or hyperindex tensors.** QXTns stores a tensor that is diagonal along a group of indices with one axis per
group (`tensor.jl` l. 55–79; `find_hyper_edges`, `reduce_tensor` in `utils.jl`), and its non-mock `contract_pair`
contracts these reduced arrays with OMEinsum, using groups connected across the network (`tensor_network.jl`
l. 244–263, 307–348, 486–505). `qctn.execute` has both: `hyper=False` (dense, one `tensordot` per step) and
`hyper=True` (the same groups, one `einsum` per step). The plan files can't tell the two apart: the ranks they print are
`QXTensor.rank`, the length of the full index list (`tensor.jl` l. 78; printed at `funcions_article_IA.jl` l. 183),
whatever the storage. Our dense ranks equal the file's on all 286,263 steps of the 109 plans. Ranks of the reduced
arrays differ on 36% of them. One plan does point to the representation: `pricingcall_…_21_pla_jdrasil` (netcon,
phase 3) needs a rank-55 dense intermediate (2^55 complex numbers), but only rank 19 (6.5 MB peak) in hyperindex
form, and the release has a measured speedup for it (1.10). So the authors' timed runs can't have been dense, which
is consistent with QXTns' hyperindex form. It is the only plan of the 17 circuits where the two forms differ that much:
the hyperindex form saves 32.1 in log2 flops there, at most 8.2 elsewhere, and 1.0 at the median.

**Our timing protocol** (`qctn.execute.timing`): leaves cast and copied to the GPU before timing; each run is the
whole plan between two CUDA events, with `Device.synchronize()` before and after; one warm-up, then up to 50 runs,
every run's time stored (`results/raw/p4/<job>/times.jsonl`) so mean, median or min can be taken later. Intermediates
come from CuPy's memory pool inside the timed region. In the small run a plan stopped repeating after 5 s of measured
time (`status` "budget", at least 2 runs). The QXTns reference (`p4_qxtns.jl`) times
`contract_tn!(copy(tn), plan)` with storage as `CuArray{ComplexF32}`, synchronised, one warm-up and 10 runs. Its
timed region includes QXTns' per-step host work, as any use of `contract_tn!` would.

**Small run: Slurm job 224785**, one Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB (driver 535.183.01), CuPy 14.2.0 with the
CUDA 12.9 runtime, both modes on the 109 release plans of the 17 QASM circuits, then QXTns on the 21 plans of
`dj_indep_qiskit_40`, `pricingcall_…_5` and `_9`. Wall time 19 min (CuPy 16 min, Julia 3 min, packages loaded in
14 s). 217 of 218 (plan, mode) pairs ran; the dense `_21_pla_jdrasil` was skipped (memory). Repeat-to-repeat
variation is small (median coefficient of variation 1.0% dense, 0.9% hyper).

Speedups against MinFill, compared with the release's labels on the 14 circuits with a MinFill plan and labels
(`p4_compare.csv`; Spearman per circuit over 4–7 plans):

| | Spearman with the labels, median (mean) | same best plan |
|---|---|---|
| CuPy dense, mean of repeats | −0.20 (−0.23) | 1 / 14 |
| CuPy hyperindex, mean | −0.29 (−0.21) | 2 / 14 |
| CuPy dense, median / min | −0.21 / −0.14 | 1 / 14 |

| plan files' mock time | 0.45 (0.36) | 6 / 14 |

Our timings don't reproduce the labels' order. What we see:
- Every plan of a circuit has the same number of steps (one per bond, phase 3), and at these sizes a run is dominated
  by the per-step cost: about 60 µs per step dense and 130 µs hyperindex (`cupy.einsum` has more overhead than
  `tensordot`), against flops that differ little between plans. Our speedups span 1.02–1.31× within a circuit,
  the labels' 1.19–2.34× (and 50× on `pricingcall_…_5`, whose `minfill_mrs` label is 38.5).
- Hyperindex runs take 1.8× as long as dense ones here, though they do fewer flops. Over all plans the two modes
  rank alike (Spearman 0.98).
- QXTns' own runs take 8–32× longer than our dense ones (0.12–0.43 s per plan against 0.010–0.047 s): its host work
  per step dominates.
- The mock times, which measure only that host work, correlate more with the labels than any real GPU timing here.
  This suggests the labels are largely host overhead of the authors' Julia stack, but three circuits of QXTns timings
  don't establish it.

**Full run, estimated** (`p4_estimate.csv`). Fitting per-run time as a + b·steps + c·flops on the 108 dense and 109
hyperindex timings (median relative error 18% and 2%), and applying it to the 1514 of the 1661 parsed plan files with
under 2^40 flops (by the files' ranks): one warm-up + 50 runs comes to 2.6 GPU-hours dense and 4.4 hyperindex. With
the small run's 5 s budget it is 1.2 and 1.7. Add CPU time to build networks and compile plans (up to 1.7 s per plan
here). The other 147 plans need 2^40 flops or more by dense count and would fail or time out. Some, like the netcon
plan above, may be feasible in hyperindex form, which only compiling them will show. The 1663 files include 102 plans
from names outside the seven generators and six that cover only part of their network (phase 3); the estimate
covers all that parse.

**Not done.** No full run. The paper's Tables 8, 9 and 11 are not compared. QXTns was timed on 3 circuits only. We did
not try to reproduce the labels with a host-overhead model.

## Phase 4b: what the labels measure

Numbers: `results/p4q_timing.csv` (one row per plan, one column per measure), `p4q_compare.csv` (per circuit and
measure), `p4q_summary.csv`, `p4q_measures.csv`, `p4q_steps.csv`, from the Julia runs
(`experiments/julia/p4q_qxtns.jl`, Slurm `scripts/slurm/p4q_julia.sbatch`) through `experiments/p4q_compare.py`; and
from the release data alone `p4q_label_stats.csv`, `p4q_label_flops.csv`, `p4q_label_noise.csv`,
`p4q_label_rescaled.csv` (`experiments/p4q_labels.py`, about 5 min) and `p4q_hyperflops.csv`
(`experiments/p4q_hyperflops.py`, about 10 min, 6 processes). Checks in `tests/test_p4q.py`. Release issues found so
far are collected in [`release-issues.md`](release-issues.md).

**Question.** Phase 4's CuPy timings ranked the plans against the labels' order (per-circuit Spearman median −0.20
dense, −0.29 hyperindex), while the plan files' mock time column ranked them with it (0.45). The hypothesis: the
labels largely measure host-side overhead of the authors' Julia stack, not GPU contraction work.

**Runs.** Slurm jobs 224825 (pilot), 224832–224835 (four circuit groups, one V100-SXM2-16GB each on four nodes,
host Xeon E5-2698 v4) and 224836 (one Julia process per plan), Julia 1.9.1 with the release's Manifest
(QXTns, OMEinsum 0.6.13, CUDA.jl 3.13.1), ComplexF32 storage on the GPU, all 109 release plans of the 17 QASM
circuits, MinFill first in each circuit. Raw runs in `results/raw/p4q/<job>/` (gitignored). Wall times 1.5–2.8 h per
group job, 2.7 h for 224836; about 12 GPU-hours in all. The other worker's GPU jobs ran on the same nodes
throughout (one or two tasks per node besides ours); host-bound timings may have been slowed by that, but the repeat
variation stayed small (coefficient of variation median 2.7%, at most 6.6%). Modes, each timed as a whole plan
between device synchronisations, every run kept:

| Mode | What runs | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| `qxtns` | QXTns `contract_tn!(tn, plan)` on `CuArray{ComplexF32}`: the library call | cold run, 1 warm-up, 50 runs (at least 42 within the 10 min per plan) |
| fresh | the same, the first contraction in a new Julia process | cold, 1 warm-up, 2 runs |
| `mock` | the same loop with `contract_pair!(...; mock=true)`: QXTns' index bookkeeping, no data | 1 + 10 |
| `authors`, `authors_mock` | the release's `contract_tn_rank_mock!` with `mock=false` / `true` (`funcions_article_IA.jl` l. 28–71) | 1 + 10 |
| `decomp` | `contract_pair!` re-implemented step for step with a synchronisation and a clock after each phase | 1 + 10 |
| `ome` | only the einsums: each step's `EinCode` on prepared operands of the step's shapes | 1 + 10 |
| CPU | `qxtns` and `ome` on `Array{ComplexF32}` | 1 + 5 |

**Where the time goes.** At these sizes QXTns' `contract_tn!` is host-bound (`p4q_timing.csv`, per circuit medians):

- 0.5–0.7 ms per step on the pricing-call circuits, 1.3–4.1 ms on the DJ and portfolio circuits (whose large
  hyperindex groups make QXTns' group search longer); 7–88× our CuPy dense times.
- The einsums alone (`ome`) take 0.6–10% of it. In `decomp`, finding the contraction indices takes 43–53% of the
  time, `tensor_data` 30–46% (it searches the hyperindex groups across the network; no operand was ever expanded or
  re-reduced: `reshaped_operands` 0 for all 109 plans), the einsum including its synchronisation 2–17%, building the
  result and updating the bond map 1–6%. The bookkeeping alone (`mock`) is 36–52% of a `qxtns` run; garbage
  collection 5–19%.
- With CPU arrays the same call takes 0.72–1.02× as long as on the GPU on 15 circuits (2.8× and 7.6× on the two
  largest), so the GPU doesn't make QXTns faster here.
- Identical plans take the same time to within 1–11% (`pricingcall_…_23`: 9.76, 9.85, 9.88 s, labels 1, 0.60, 0.83;
  `_25`: 13.1, 14.2, 14.6 s, labels 0.55, 0.57, 0.45), later plans in a session slightly slower.
- The release's mock column is reproduced step by step: on all 109 plans our run of `contract_tn_rank_mock!(…;
  mock=true)` gives per-step times with a median step-by-step Spearman of 0.93 (0.73–0.98) against the file's, and
  a median step of 286 µs against 312 µs (`p4q_steps.csv`). The file's totals contain more outlier steps (median 8
  steps above 10× the plan's median step; the largest single step 1.43 s), consistent with compilation and garbage
  collection in a long session.

Per circuit (`p4q_compare.csv`) QXTns agrees best on the DJ and portfolio circuits (Spearman 0.43–0.82, same best
plan on all four) and least on `pricingcall_…_11`–`_21` (−0.29 to 0). Our measures fall in two groups that agree
within themselves and not with each other (`p4q_measures.csv`, median within-circuit Spearman): the host-bound ones
(`qxtns`, `authors`, `mock`, CPU `qxtns`: 0.75–0.93 between them) and the GPU-work ones (`ome`, CuPy: 0.43–0.57),
with −0.2 to 0.2 between the groups. The release's mock column agrees with our host-bound totals only at 0.2–0.4,
although it matches our mock run step by step: within a circuit our mock totals span a factor of 1.35 (median,
slowest over fastest plan), while the file's outlier steps make its sums span 2.9 (median; up to 14).

**Against the labels, all 223 circuits** (release data only, `p4q_label_stats.csv`, `p4q_label_flops.csv`). The
labels correlate with the plan files' median mock time per step (Spearman of −time median 0.36, mean 0.29) and sum
(0.20), and not with the work: log2 flops 0.04 (dense) and 0.11 (hyperindex), where a GPU-bound measurement would give
a clearly negative value; no single feature exceeds 0.26 in absolute value. Over pairs of plans of one circuit, the
plan with more hyperindex flops has the lower speedup in 39–47% of pairs at any flop difference up to 2^20. Where the
larger plan has at least 2^36 flops, it is 60–65%, with a median speedup ratio of 1.02–1.12 for flop differences of
2^1–2^11. In hyperindex form 1379 of the 1463 split plans have under 2^36 flops (counted as index combinations;
with 8 real operations each, under 40 ms at the V100's peak FP32 rate, under 15 ms at the A6000's), the heaviest
2^41.2 (1.4 s and 0.5 s). Host overhead at 0.5–4 ms per step over hundreds to thousands of steps is 0.1 s to tens of
seconds, so for nearly every split plan the overhead is larger than the arithmetic in this stack.

**How consistent the labels are** (`p4q_label_noise.csv`). The 17 MinFill copies and the 12 repeated listings are
identical plans with their own speedups. From the copies, one measurement's log speedup has a noise level of
σ ≈ 0.097 (robust estimate; 0.082 without the pricing-call circuits, whose copies are all slower than MinFill,
0.45–0.97). A simulated re-measurement with that noise would agree with the labels to a within-circuit Spearman of
0.64 (median, 223 circuits; 0.62 on the 15 QASM circuits) and pick the same best plan in 40% of circuits. No
timing can be expected to reproduce the labels better than that.

**MinFill normalisation** (`p4q_label_rescaled.csv`). In the 21 circuits with a MinFill speedup other than 1 that
`measures.xlsx` covers, every split speedup is `measures.xlsx`'s times one constant, the MinFill row's split value
(e.g. 686.13 for `QFT_6`, 0.546 for `RQC_4_5_39`). `measures.xlsx` has MinFill at 1 in all its circuits. So these
circuits' order is the same in both files and only the normaliser differs; where it came from isn't in the release.

**What this shows.**

- Supported, moderately: on these circuits the labels follow host-side overhead of the authors' stack and not GPU
  arithmetic. The library call, its index and `tensor_data` phases, our run of the release's mock function and the
  release's mock column all correlate positively with the labels (means 0.28–0.46, 95% intervals above 0); the other
  host-bound variants (bookkeeping alone, the release's non-mock function, CPU arrays, warm runs in a fresh process)
  have positive means of 0.10–0.17 with intervals that include 0. Every GPU-work measure (einsums alone, CuPy,
  flops) has a negative mean (−0.14 to −0.18). Over all 223 circuits the labels are unrelated to flops in either
  form and related to the mock bookkeeping column. The authors' own library call, timed with the paper's protocol on
  a V100, ranks plans like the labels with a mean Spearman of 0.28, about half of what a re-measurement of the labels
  would give (0.57), and better than any GPU-work timing.
- Not established: that the labels are exactly QXTns `contract_tn!` times. Our host is an Intel Xeon E5-2698 v4, theirs
  an AMD EPYC 7282; host-bound times can rank plans differently on another CPU, and the session order, garbage
  collection and the GPU (RTX A6000) are unknown. With 15 circuits of 3–7 plans and label noise of about 10% per plan,
  the differences between the host-overhead measures (0.28–0.46 in mean Spearman) are within the uncertainty.
- Not supported: compilation as the source. First runs, in a session or in a fresh process, include up to 125 s of
  compilation and rank plans unlike the labels.
- So the speedups in the splits mostly reflect how long QXTns' index and hyperindex bookkeeping takes per plan, which
  depends on the network's hyperindex groups and the ranks of the intermediates, plus noise of about 10% per plan. A
  ranker trained on them learns that, which may explain why it does well on these labels with features that don't
  include the flop count (`log2_sum_flops` was discarded, Sec. 5.3).

**Not done.** Timing on an AMD host or an RTX A6000. Plans outside the 17 QASM circuits (the other worker's full run
covers CuPy only). A model of the bookkeeping cost per step from the network's hyperindex groups. A session that runs
the circuits in the authors' order, which the release doesn't record.
